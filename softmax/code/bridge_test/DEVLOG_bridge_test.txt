CONSEQUENCE 1 -- THE EARLIER NUMBERS ARE RETROACTIVELY LICENSED. The harness
folds a 56-mer to plddt 93.2 with a clean clash record through the SAME code
path, the same init_structure_config(), the same decoder and the same CB
inference used for every zero_polymer run. So
condition_on_coordinates_only=True is EXONERATED as a suspect -- it was the
named first suspect and it is not the problem. Therefore ptm=0.232 (cleaved)
and 0.261 (mature), the 95.7 / 129.7 A bridge separations and the 16 / 9
CB-CB clashes are properties of what ESM3 does with THIS 550-residue HA
construct, not artefacts of our plumbing. The previous entry's conclusions
stand, and may now be stated without the "void until the control passes"
hedge.

CONSEQUENCE 2 -- CAVEAT (a) IS RETRACTED. The previous entry flagged that the
clash count might be a glycine/CB artefact. Reading the provenance settled
it: ProteinChain.infer_cbeta() derives CB from N/CA/C and, with its default
infer_cbeta_for_glycine=False, writes NaN at every glycine, so glycines were
excluded from every CB statistic by construction all along. The control now
VERIFIES that rather than arguing it: "CB absent (NaN) at 4 residues; 4
glycines in sequence" -- the two counts agree exactly, so no CB is missing
for any other reason and every clash counted anywhere is between two real
inferred CBs. Since inferred_cbeta is a deterministic function of the
backbone, two finite CBs 1.2 A apart mean two nearly superimposed BACKBONES.
The HA clashes are real geometry violations.

CONSEQUENCE 3 -- THE CLASH THRESHOLD IS NOW CALIBRATED, not assumed. CB_CLASH
= 3.0 A was picked from first principles (vdW contact ~3.4 A, C-C bond 1.54
A). The control supplies the empirical side: a fold ESM3 is confident about
has its closest non-adjacent CB pair at 3.68 A, i.e. ABOVE the cutoff with
margin and without any tuning. The HA runs sit at 1.17-1.50 A. So 3.0 A
separates "confident fold" from "broken geometry" cleanly on the two cases we
have, and the gate is not merely a threshold we invented to fail HA.

ALSO FIXED in caeb436, found while reading the CB provenance: the chainbreak
is not a residue, but "|" is not glycine either, so infer_cbeta hands it a CB
derived from meaningless backbone, and the earlier runs let that position into
the CB matrix, the clash count AND the minimum. A residue mask now excludes
it. The published cleaved figures (min CB-CB 1.50 A, 16 clashes) may therefore
shift slightly on a re-run; the mature figures cannot, since that reference
has no break. label() was also wrong for any break-free reference -- with no
"|", find() returns -1 and every HA2 residue was labelled one:329+n, which is
why the first mature table had to be hand-translated. Both now derive from
chain_spans().

STILL OPEN, and what the two pending 256-step re-runs decide: whether HA's
failure is the obligate-trimer explanation. The discriminator is PER-CHAIN
plddt, now reported:
  - plddt high (>~70) within polymer_one and polymer_two while ptm stays
    ~0.23  =>  the two chains FOLD and their RELATIVE PLACEMENT fails. That is
    the signature of an obligate trimer whose HA2 central helix is an
    inter-protomer coiled-coil, and it means the bridge question needs the
    trimer, not more steps.
  - plddt low everywhere  =>  nothing folds, and the 550-residue HA ectodomain
    is simply outside what esm3-open does unconditioned.
Either way the answer is about the target. What it is NOT about: the
chainbreak (exonerated), the step budget (1 -> 256 bought +0.05 ptm), the CB
inference (verified), or the harness (this entry).

FINDING 1 -- THE CHAINBREAK IS BENEFICIAL, not merely harmless. The previous
entry could only say it was exonerated, because ptm alone slightly FAVOURS
mature (0.261 vs 0.232). Per-chain plddt reverses that and is far more
substantial than the 0.03 of ptm:
    HA1 plddt        51.42 -> 63.22   (+11.8)
    HA1 confident     11.2% -> 38.6%  (3.4x more residues above 70)
    HA2 plddt        70.78 -> 64.90   (-5.9)
    overall          59.20 -> 63.89   (+4.7), 31.8% -> 45.5% confident
So telling ESM3 where the cut is helps HA1 a great deal, costs HA2 a little,
and nets clearly positive. Marco's original premise -- that the two pieces
should go in as one molecule rather than as separate monomers -- is
vindicated as far as this evidence goes: the model does something better with
the break than without it.

FINDING 2 -- IT HELPS EXACTLY WHERE THE BRIDGE IS. plddt at Cys14 goes
22.5 -> 73.6. In the break-free form HA1's N-terminal region is essentially
unplaced (22.5 is noise), which is why mature's bridge separation is the
absurd 129.7 A: the anchor is flailing, not merely misplaced. With the break
the same anchor is confidently positioned (73.6). The bridge is still not
formed, but one of its two ends stops being garbage.

FINDING 3 -- THE CLASH TOPOLOGY DIFFERS QUALITATIVELY, and this is the
sharper signal, which the raw count hides (mature has FEWER clashes, 9 vs
15, and is nonetheless much worse):
    cleaved  |i-j| = 3, 4, 4, 16, 2, 2, 10, 2        -- all LOCAL
    mature   |i-j| = 140, 103, 159, 92, 2, 135, 80, 90 -- mostly LONG-RANGE
Local clashes are sloppy backbone geometry over a few residues. Long-range
clashes at |i-j| ~ 100-160 mean two distant segments of the chain are
SUPERIMPOSED -- the chain passes through itself, which is topologically
impossible rather than merely imprecise. mature is globally wrong; cleaved is
locally rough. Reading clash counts without the separation distribution would
have ranked these backwards. Standing lesson: report |i-j| with every clash.

FINDING 4 -- THE PRE-REGISTERED TRIMER SIGNATURE IS NOT MET. The discriminator
set out in the previous entry wanted BOTH chains above ~70 with ptm still low
("domains fold, assembly fails"). Actual result is neither branch: the deficit
is ASYMMETRIC. HA2 is adequate in both runs (64.9 / 70.8, roughly 60% of
residues confident); HA1 is poor in both and dreadful without the break (63.2
/ 51.4, 38.6% / 11.2%). So it is not "both pieces fold but cannot be placed",
and not "nothing folds" either. The obligate-trimer explanation is NOT dead
-- HA1 is the receptor-binding head, and heads do pack against neighbouring
protomers in the native trimer -- but it is no longer the only reading, and
the data localise the problem to HA1 specifically rather than to the
assembly as a whole.

THE MAIN QUESTION REMAINS UNANSWERED, and honestly so. Both folds fail the
gate (ptm 0.23 / 0.26, real clashes), so neither licenses a statement about
whether ESM3 finds the disulfide. Bridge CB-CB is 95.7 / 129.7 A against a
4.5 A criterion, ranked 98th / 95th of 136 cysteine pairs. What can be said:
the failure is not the harness (controlled), not the chainbreak (it helps),
not the CB inference (42 NaN = 42 glycines, exactly, in both runs), and not
the step budget in any cheap way.

Minor: excluding the chainbreak position dropped cleaved's clash count 16 ->
15, so exactly one of the originally reported clashes was the bogus CB
inferred at "|". The 1.50 A minimum is unchanged, so the break was never the
extreme. mature is unaffected, as predicted, having no break.

A 29-point plddt drop from adding HA1. So the 550-residue construct is WORSE
THAN ITS PART for the half that can fold on its own, and HA2's plddt alone
(93.93) is statistically indistinguishable from protein_g's (93.24) -- this

This sharpens FINDING 1 without overturning it. The break still helps HA1
(51.4 -> 63.2) and still costs HA2 (70.8 -> 64.9), so the break is the better
way to present the pair. But the pair itself is the problem: both chains in
company sit far below what HA2 reaches alone.

FINDING 6 -- THE BRIDGE CRITERION HAS NO CONTROL, and this is a hole in MY
experiment design, not in the data. In a fold at plddt 93.9 with zero
clashes, the closest cysteine pair is 6.26 A CB-CB and NOT ONE of HA2's 28
pairs falls inside the 4.5 A criterion. HA2 carries 8 cysteines, which in the
native protein form intrachain disulfides. So either ESM3 systematically
fails to tighten disulfides in this backbone+CB output, or HA2's disulfides
genuinely are not formed even when the fold is confident.

The geometry narrows it. For a bonded pair, CB-SG is ~1.81 A and SG-SG ~2.05
A, so a fully extended collinear CB-SG-SG-CB gives a HARD CEILING of
    1.81 + 2.05 + 1.81 = 5.67 A
and real disulfides, whose CB-SG-SG-CB dihedral sits near +/-90 degrees, come
in at 3.8-4.1 A. 6.26 A is therefore beyond the geometric maximum for a
bonded pair, whatever threshold one picks: that pair is NOT a disulfide in
this structure. And since CB here is inferred from N/CA/C by ideal geometry,
CB error IS backbone error, which at plddt 94 is well under the ~2 A
discrepancy involved. So this is not imprecision -- ESM3's confident HA2 fold
simply does not place these cysteines in bonding geometry.

CONSEQUENCE FOR EVERY "bridge NOT formed" VERDICT SO FAR: they may be
measured against a threshold this model never meets. If ESM3 never resolves a
disulfide to 4.5 A even where one certainly exists, then 95.7 A and 129.7 A
remain damning (they are 20x the ceiling) but a near-miss result could never
have been detected, and the criterion cannot distinguish "ESM3 declined to
form the bridge" from "ESM3 cannot express a bridge".

protein_g COULD NOT HAVE CAUGHT THIS: it has no cysteines, which the control
output notes approvingly. A control that validates the fold but not the
MEASUREMENT was only ever half a control.

REQUIRED NEXT, before any further bridge claim: a DISULFIDE-CONTAINING
positive control -- a small, disulfide-rich protein ESM3 folds confidently,
through this same pipeline, with its known pairs checked.
  - known disulfides come out at 3.8-4.5 A  =>  the criterion is sound, and
    HA2's cysteines genuinely are unbonded in these predictions.
  - they come out at 6-7 A                  =>  ESM3 under-tightens
    disulfides as a rule, CB_BONDED must be recalibrated to that scale, and
    every verdict in this log is re-read against the new number.
Deliberately NOT choosing the sequence from memory here: typing a reference
sequence from recall is precisely the class of error that produced the
Arg329/Cys14 off-by-16, and it would be worse in a control whose whole job is
to calibrate the yardstick. Sequence to be supplied or taken from a named PDB
entry and checked before it enters references.py.
