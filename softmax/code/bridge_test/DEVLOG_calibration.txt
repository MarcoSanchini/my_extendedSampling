2026-10-09 -- DISULFIDE CALIBRATION: BPTI is clean. Crambin shows the 4.5 A criterion has SENSITIVITY but no SPECIFICITY. A distance threshold cannot be the bridge verdict on its own
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Two controls with known disulfides, run at full step budget through the same
pipeline as every other result. The fold gate passes for both, so the
measurements are licensed; only the interpretation is in question.

FINDING 1 -- THE 4.5 A CRITERION IS NOT WHAT FAILS IN BPTI. Its three true
bonds sit at 3.60-4.45 A, and the first false pair is 1.7 A further out, at
6.15 A. In the protein where the model is most confident about the bonds, the
threshold separates them cleanly. The range 3.6-4.5 A is achievable for a real
bond in this pipeline. That part of the earlier conclusion survives.

FINDING 2 -- CRAMBIN FAILS IN TWO OPPOSITE WAYS, and this is the important
result. (a) MISSED BONDS: Cys3-Cys40 and Cys16-Cys26 are real bonds that the
model places 7.75 and 10.42 A apart. (b) FALSE BONDS: Cys16-Cys40 is the
closest pair in the whole molecule at 3.33 A, inside the criterion, and is not
a native bond. A distance threshold therefore has sensitivity that depends on
the protein (3/3 in BPTI, 1/3 in crambin) and no specificity at all, since it
accepts a non-native pair as readily as a native one.

Consequence for every bridge verdict: "formed" cannot mean "inside 4.5 A". It
needs the partner identity as well, and that requires a verified native list
for the molecule in question. Distance is necessary for a bond, not
sufficient.

FINDING 3 -- MY EARLIER WORDING WAS TOO STRONG, and I am retracting it. The
calibration script printed, for BPTI alone, "CB_BONDED = 4.5 A IS SOUND ...
HA2's disulfides genuinely are NOT formed". That treated sensitivity in one
protein as if it validated the criterion, and it did not test specificity at
all. Crambin is the first run that shows the other side. The 6.26 A closest
pair in HA2 is still a measured number; the inference drawn from it is
weaker than the devlog said.

FINDING 4 -- FINDING 9 OF THE BRIDGE DEVLOG OVERSTATED. It described HA1-alone
pairs at 3.51 A (one:277-281) and 3.92 A (one:64-76) as "inside real disulfide
geometry". They are inside the distance range, which is necessary, and by
crambin's Cys16-Cys40 they could equally be non-native. They are withdrawn as
evidence of native bonds. They remain an observation that ESM3 places those
cysteines close together in the HA1-alone fold, which is all they show.

FINDING 5 -- WHAT SURVIVES. The bridge verdicts that rest on large separations
do not depend on specificity. The cleaved bridge (Cys14-Cys137) at 95.7 A and
the mature bridge at 129.7 A are around 20 times the criterion, so no
specificity question can rescue them. The result that the bridge is not formed
in the cleaved and mature constructs at 256 steps stands.

The HA2-alone result is weaker. No pair falls within 4.5 A in the one
gate-passing HA2 fold. Given that BPTI does place its native bonds in range,
and crambin places only one of three, this reads as a real but soft signal
that ESM3 is not forming HA2's bonds with confidence. It is not proof that the
HA2 cysteines are unbonded in nature, and it cannot be strengthened without a
verified native list for HA.

FINDING 6 -- THE HA PARTNER QUESTION CANNOT BE ASKED YET. For HA we have no
verified native disulfide list in references.py. Without one, partner
recovery, which is the test crambin just exposed as the weak point, cannot be
run on HA at all. That is the same gap that the BPTI and crambin controls
closed for the calibration, and it is needed before any positive bridge claim
about HA. Any list must come from a named structure entry and be checked
against its sequence before it enters references.py. It must not be recalled.

OPEN, and the next run that would settle the crambin misses:
  - Per-residue pLDDT at each known-bond residue. The calibration block does
    not print it yet, so the crambin misses are not yet attributable. If
    Cys3, Cys16, Cys26 and Cys40 sit in low-confidence residues, the misses
    are uncertainty; if they are confident, the placement is wrong. These two
    readings call for different responses.
  - The calibration verdict should report specificity as well as sensitivity:
    the count of in-range non-native pairs alongside the count of in-range
    known bonds. Currently only sensitivity is printed, which is how BPTI
    came to look like a clean pass.

Neither change requires new science, only the two reruns above and a verdict
rule that counts both sides.
